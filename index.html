About

Why Biomedical Sciences?

My journey from insect enthusiast to aspiring biomedical researcher.

From Insects to Genes

At age 6, I became fascinated by insects — beetles, mantises, and ants — each with their own unique behaviours and social structures. This curiosity naturally extended to the science of life, leading me to explore how genes shape behaviour and disease.

The Selfish Gene Perspective

Reading Richard Dawkins' The Selfish Gene shifted my view of biology: organisms are vehicles for genes. Watching my ants sacrifice themselves for the colony made this tangible — a powerful lens for understanding cancer and evolution.

PCR — From Invisible to Visible

Performing PCR transformed biology from descriptive to investigative. Watching DNA amplify — invisible to visible — I realized we can read and write the code of life. This is the future I want to be part of.

Public Health & Policy

The pandemic showed me that biology isn't just about individual cells — it's about populations. Biomedical knowledge must inform real-world policy. I aim to bridge this gap through research and translation.
